# Privacy and freedom: the common questions

## "I have nothing to hide"
Most people close the bathroom door, seal their letters and draw the curtains
at night, not from guilt but because their lives are their own. Privacy is
not a confession that something is wrong; it is the ordinary boundary that
lets a person be themselves.

## Why financial privacy matters
What you pay for describes your life: your health, your faith, your
politics, the people you love, the causes you give to. A public ledger of
every payment would let anyone, from a curious neighbour to a thief, read
that life. Cash has always offered privacy; Zcash offers the same dignity
for digital money.

## Privacy and freedom
Freedom of thought and association depend on being able to act without being
watched. People who know they are observed choose differently; they
self-censor. Privacy protects the space in which free choices are made.

## The freedom to transact
The foundation on which our core freedoms are built is the freedom to transact.
To be debanked is to be forcibly removed from society. To speak without the risk of
prosecution is insufficient for true freedom. One must be able to speak without the risk
of persecution. There is no true freedom without the freedom to transact.

## Privacy is not lawlessness
Zcash's viewing keys let an owner disclose their activity to an accountant
or a tax authority whenever they choose. Privacy and accountability are
compatible: the point is that the owner decides who sees, not that no one
ever can.
