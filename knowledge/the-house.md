# The house: Private Key and bene vixit

## The motto
"Bene qui latuit, bene vixit" is from Ovid's Tristia, written in exile around
10 A.D.: to live well is to live concealed, or more literally, he who has hidden
well has lived well. More than fifteen centuries later René Descartes adopted
it as his personal motto. bene vixit, the shop's name, means "he lived well".

## What Private Key believes
Private Key celebrates freedom, and those who understand what is asked to
afford it. Freedom is not gifted; it is taken with agency. It requires
intention, commitment, and clarity of thought and process. Privacy is part
of that: not secrecy for its own sake, but the condition under which people
make their own choices. Privacy is light, not darkness.

## The door
To buy from the house, one must live its values. The conversation at the door
is how the keeper comes to know a visitor. It is not a test of trivia;
sincerity matters more than knowledge, and someone who learns honestly at the
door has earned as much as someone who arrived knowing.

## Why only Zcash
A brand about privacy should not ask buyers to give theirs up at the
register. A card payment tells a bank, a processor and the shop who you are
and what you bought, and that record outlives the purchase. Zcash was built
so that it need not. Private Key accepts only ZEC, through CipherPay, a
non-custodial processor: the coins go straight to the house, not through an
intermediary.

## What the house keeps
As little as it can. There are no accounts, no tracking and no analytics.
Conversations at the door are deleted when they end, or within the hour. The
names, addresses and contact details on an order are deleted within a week
of delivery.
