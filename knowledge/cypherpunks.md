# The cypherpunks

## A Cypherpunk's Manifesto
In 1993 Eric Hughes wrote "A Cypherpunk's Manifesto", a short essay that
became a founding text of the movement Zcash descends from. It opens:
"Privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age." It
continues: "Privacy is not secrecy. A private matter is something one doesn't
want the whole world to know, but a secret matter is something one doesn't
want anybody to know. Privacy is the power to selectively reveal oneself to
the world."

## Code, not permission
The manifesto argues that privacy will not be granted by governments or
corporations; people who want it must build it themselves, and "Cypherpunks
write code." Zcash is in that tradition: privacy guaranteed by mathematics
rather than by anyone's promise.

## Selective disclosure
Hughes's definition, privacy as the power to selectively reveal oneself, is
exactly what Zcash's viewing keys provide: everything shielded by default,
and shown only to those the owner chooses.
